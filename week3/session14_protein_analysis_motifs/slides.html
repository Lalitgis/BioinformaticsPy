Protein physicochemical properties

from Bio.SeqUtils.ProtParam import ProteinAnalysis

pa = ProteinAnalysis(str(protein_seq))
print("Molecular weight:", pa.molecular_weight())
print("Aromaticity:", pa.aromaticity())
print("Instability index:", pa.instability_index())
print("Isoelectric point:", pa.isoelectric_point())
print("Amino acid percent:", pa.get_amino_acids_percent())
Isoelectric point (pI) and instability index are used e.g. to predict how a protein will behave on a 2D gel, or whether a designed transgene protein is likely to be stable when expressed in a plant.

Motifs: short, functionally meaningful patterns

A sequence motif is a short conserved pattern associated with a specific structural or functional role — often written as a regular expression allowing some variable positions.

MotifPatternFunction
Walker A / P-loopG.{4}GK[ST]ATP/GTP binding — found in NB-ARC domains of plant disease-resistance (R) proteins
Kinase-2-like[LIVMF]{2}D[DE]-ishMg2+ coordination in NB-ARC
import re
match = re.search(r"G.{4}GK[ST]", protein_seq)
if match:
    print("P-loop motif found at position", match.start())

The NBS-LRR family — plants' innate immune receptors

NBS-LRR (NB-ARC + Leucine-Rich Repeat) proteins are the largest class of plant disease-resistance (R) genes — hundreds per genome in most crops. The N-terminal NB-ARC domain acts as a molecular "on/off switch" (binds ATP/ADP), while the C-terminal LRR domain provides pathogen-effector recognition specificity. Famous examples: tomato Mi-1.2 (nematode resistance), rice Xa21 (bacterial blight resistance), potato Rpi-blb2 (late blight resistance — the pathogen behind the Irish Potato Famine).

Today's dataset contains 4 synthetic teaching proteins engineered with a realistic P-loop motif and LRR-like repeats, modeled on this real gene family.

Scanning a protein family for a motif

from Bio import SeqIO
import re

for record in SeqIO.parse("NBS_LRR_resistance_proteins.fasta", "fasta"):
    seq = str(record.seq)
    match = re.search(r"G.{4}GK[ST]", seq)
    status = f"P-loop at pos {match.start()}" if match else "no P-loop found"
    print(f"{record.id:30s} {status}")
This exact pattern — scan many sequences, flag which carry a functional motif — is how researchers do first-pass candidate-gene screening in a newly sequenced plant genome before any expensive wet-lab work begins.
