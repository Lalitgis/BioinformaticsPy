Why bioinformatics, why plants?

Bioinformatics is the use of computation to store, search, and interpret biological data — DNA/RNA/protein sequences, genome annotations, expression measurements, and genetic variation.

🌾 Plant angle: Plant biology generates enormous, messy datasets fast: crop genomes (many are huge and highly repetitive/polyploid — wheat is hexaploid!), germplasm collections with thousands of accessions, RNA-seq under stress treatments (drought, salinity, pathogens), and marker-assisted breeding programs. A plant breeder or physiologist who can script is a huge advantage.

  • Genomics — sequencing and comparing whole genomes (e.g., rice vs its wild relatives).
  • Transcriptomics — which genes turn on/off under drought, cold, pathogen attack.
  • Population genetics — SNPs across germplasm panels for breeding & GWAS.
  • Phylogenetics — evolutionary relationships among crop species and wild relatives.

The Central Dogma — the spine of everything we'll code

DNA → (transcription) → RNA → (translation) → Protein. Every Biopython exercise in this course is, underneath, manipulating one of these three molecules or comparing many copies of them across species/individuals.

MoleculeAlphabetBiopython objectPlant example
DNAA,C,G,TBio.Seq.SeqFLC gene controlling flowering time in Arabidopsis
RNAA,C,G,USeq.transcribe()mRNA of a drought-responsive DREB gene
Protein20 amino acidsSeq.translate()NBS-LRR disease-resistance protein
A gene = a stretch of DNA. Its CDS (coding sequence) is the part that gets translated, starting at ATG and ending at a stop codon (TAA/TAG/TGA). Everything else in the transcript (UTRs, introns) is regulatory or gets spliced out.

Genome scale — why plants are a special computational challenge

SpeciesGenome sizePloidyWhy it matters
Arabidopsis thaliana~135 Mbdiploidsmall model plant — great for learning
Oryza sativa (rice)~430 Mbdiploidmajor crop, well-annotated reference
Zea mays (maize)~2.3 Gbdiploid, very repetitive~85% transposable elements!
Triticum aestivum (bread wheat)~16 Gbhexaploid (6 copies)3 sub-genomes to disentangle

This is why we start on tiny, clean examples (single genes, short regions) before ever touching a whole crop genome — the biological logic is identical, only the scale changes.
