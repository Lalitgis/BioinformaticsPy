# Session 1 — Exercise: "Map the Dogma"

No coding today. Hand this out as a **worksheet / group discussion** (15–20 min).

## Task
For each plant scenario below, identify which molecule (DNA, RNA, or protein) is
most directly responsible, and name the stage of the central dogma involved.

1. A rice variety has a mutation in the *SD1* gene that makes it shorter (semi-dwarf,
   part of the Green Revolution). *Which molecule was originally altered?*
2. Under drought stress, a plant's cells rapidly produce more DREB2A transcription
   factor protein within a few hours. *What two processes had to happen for the
   protein amount to increase?*
3. Two tomato varieties have identical DNA sequence at a gene, but one produces
   much more protein from it because of a mutation in its promoter (not the CDS).
   *Does this change the protein sequence itself? Why or why not?*
4. An NBS-LRR disease-resistance gene is transcribed into mRNA, but a large intron
   must be removed before translation. *Where in the cell does splicing happen,
   and what would happen if it failed?*

## Discussion questions
- Why do bioinformaticians usually store *genomic* DNA, *mRNA/cDNA*, and
  *protein* sequences in separate files/formats?
- Why might two genes with very similar DNA sequences produce proteins with
  very different functions? (Hint: think about where the differences fall.)

## Answer key (for the tutor)
1. DNA (a coding mutation in the gene itself, affecting the GA-signalling pathway).
2. Transcription (DNA→mRNA) then translation (mRNA→protein) — both must increase.
3. No — a promoter mutation changes *how much* mRNA/protein is made (expression
   level), not the protein's amino acid sequence, since the CDS is unchanged.
4. Splicing happens in the nucleus; if it failed, the intron would remain in the
   mRNA, very likely introducing a premature stop codon and a non-functional
   truncated protein.
