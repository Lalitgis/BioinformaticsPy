The three GO namespaces

NamespaceAnswersExample plant term
Molecular Function (MF)What does the gene product do biochemically? "DNA-binding transcription factor activity"
Biological Process (BP)What larger process is it part of? "response to water deprivation"
Cellular Component (CC)Where in the cell does it act? "nucleus", "chloroplast thylakoid membrane"

GO terms form a hierarchy (a directed acyclic graph) — e.g. "response to water deprivation" is a child of the broader "response to abiotic stimulus."

Assigning functional labels from what we already know

We already have real biological knowledge about the genes in our drought-stress dataset from earlier sessions. Let's formalize it into a simple annotation dictionary — exactly the kind of manually-curated starting point real databases like TAIR (The Arabidopsis Information Resource) began from decades ago.

go_annotation = {
    "DREB2A": {"BP": "response to water deprivation", "MF": "DNA-binding transcription factor activity"},
    "RD29A":  {"BP": "response to water deprivation", "MF": "molecular function unknown"},
    "RBCS1A": {"BP": "photosynthesis, light reaction", "MF": "monooxygenase activity"},
    "CAB1":   {"BP": "photosynthesis, light harvesting", "MF": "chlorophyll binding"},
    "ACTIN2": {"BP": "cytoskeleton organization", "MF": "structural constituent of cytoskeleton"},
    # ... etc for all genes
}

A simple enrichment check by hand

Suppose a Session-18-style differential expression analysis flags 6 "up under drought" genes. Are "response to water deprivation" genes over-represented in that list compared to all 20 genes in our panel?

def enrichment_check(gene_list, go_annotation, term, all_genes):
    hits_in_list = sum(1 for g in gene_list if go_annotation.get(g, {}).get("BP") == term)
    hits_in_background = sum(1 for g in all_genes if go_annotation.get(g, {}).get("BP") == term)
    frac_list = hits_in_list / len(gene_list)
    frac_background = hits_in_background / len(all_genes)
    return frac_list, frac_background
This manual approach is for building intuition only — a real enrichment analysis (Fisher's exact test via tools like topGO, g:Profiler, or PANTHER) computes a proper p-value accounting for list size and background size, and corrects for testing many GO terms at once (multiple-testing correction).

Why this matters for plant biology

When a real RNA-seq study finds 500 genes upregulated under drought, no human can manually read 500 gene descriptions. GO enrichment answers "what's the story?" in one glance: e.g. "genes involved in response to water deprivation and abscisic acid signaling are massively over-represented" — turning a long gene list into a biological narrative you can put in a paper's abstract.
