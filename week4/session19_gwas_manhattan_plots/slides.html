GWAS in one paragraph

A GWAS tests, across the whole genome, whether each SNP's genotype statistically correlates with a trait of interest (e.g., plant height, yield, disease resistance) across a diversity panel of many individuals/accessions. Each SNP gets its own p-value; SNPs with very low p-values are candidates for being near (or in) a gene that actually influences the trait.

The rice SD1 semi-dwarfing gene (part of the Green Revolution) and the rice Sub1A submergence-tolerance gene were both confirmed/refined using exactly this kind of association approach in real rice diversity panels.

From p-values to a Manhattan plot

import pandas as pd
import numpy as np

gwas = pd.read_csv("rice_plant_height_gwas.csv")
gwas["p_value"] = gwas["p_value"].astype(float)
gwas["neg_log10_p"] = -np.log10(gwas["p_value"])

We plot -log10(p) instead of raw p-values because it turns "smaller is more significant" into "higher is more significant" (a p-value of 0.00001 becomes a tall, easy-to-spot peak at 5), and compresses an enormous numeric range into something visually manageable.

Building the plot, colored by chromosome

import matplotlib
matplotlib.use("Agg")
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

gwas = gwas.sort_values(["chrom", "pos"]).reset_index(drop=True)
gwas["plot_pos"] = range(len(gwas))   # cumulative x-axis position across all chromosomes

colors = ["#4a90d9", "#c98b3a"]  # alternate colors per chromosome
plt.figure(figsize=(12, 4))
for i, chrom in enumerate(sorted(gwas["chrom"].unique())):
    subset = gwas[gwas["chrom"] == chrom]
    plt.scatter(subset["plot_pos"], subset["neg_log10_p"], s=6, color=colors[i % 2])

plt.axhline(-np.log10(5e-8), color="red", linestyle="--", label="genome-wide significance")
plt.xlabel("Position across genome (by chromosome)")
plt.ylabel("-log10(p-value)")
plt.legend()
plt.savefig("manhattan_plot.png", dpi=150)

Interpreting the peak

Today's dataset simulates a rice plant-height GWAS with one strong, realistic association peak on chromosome 1 (modeled loosely on the real SD1 semi-dwarf locus region). Your exercise: find the single most significant SNP, report its position, and discuss what a breeder would do next (e.g., fine-mapping, candidate gene search in that window, marker development for MAS — tying directly back to the CAPS marker concepts from Session 13!).
A GWAS peak points to a genomic region, not a single proven causal gene — due to linkage disequilibrium (nearby SNPs are inherited together), the true causal variant could be anywhere in a window of tens to hundreds of kilobases around the peak.
